Characterizing Human Semantic Navigation as Trajectories in Embedding Space

Felipe D. Toro-Hernández1*, Jesuino Vieira Filho2*, Rodrigo M. Cabral-Carvalho1*
1 Center of Mathematics, Computing & Cognition · Federal University of ABC
2 Dept. of Computer Science & Operations Research · Université de Montréal
*Equal Contribution
Paper Code OpenReview

We frame human semantic retrieval as continuous trajectories in embedding space. Each point encodes the cumulative history of all words produced so far, enabling physics-inspired metrics that capture navigation dynamics beyond binary clustering.

Abstract

Human semantic navigation — how people retrieve and organize concepts from memory — is typically analyzed through clustering and switching in verbal fluency tasks. These binary characterizations miss the continuous, step-by-step geometry of how meaning unfolds over time.

We propose a geometric framework that treats semantic retrieval as trajectories through embedding space. By cumulatively encoding each participant's word sequence, we compute five physics-inspired metrics — distance to next, velocity, acceleration, entropy, and distance to centroid — that capture the fine-grained dynamics of semantic search.

Across four datasets spanning four languages (Spanish, English, Italian, German), two task types, and three embedding models, our framework (i) distinguishes neurodegenerative patients from healthy controls via kinematic signatures of executive dysfunction, (ii) discriminates semantic categories and reveals language-specific organization, and (iii) produces convergent results across embedding architectures for local dynamics. Cumulative encoding excels for long trajectories while non-cumulative representations are better suited for short sequences — offering a practical guideline for researchers.

Key Idea: Cumulative Embeddings

Each word xt encodes the full prefix up to step t.

Step 1: embed "cat"
Step 2: embed "cat dog"
Step 3: embed "cat dog shark"
  ⋮

This captures dependencies between successive items — semantic retrieval is inherently cumulative.

Result: a unique trajectory per participant × concept pair, encoding how meaning unfolds over time.

Five Physics-Inspired Trajectory Metrics

① Distance to Next

Cosine distance between consecutive points. Semantic jump size.

② Velocity

vt = xt+1 − xt — Direction and magnitude of each step.

③ Acceleration

at = vt+1 − vt — Low → stable cluster. High → erratic switch.

④ Entropy

Shannon entropy of median-split steps. Measures predictability of the semantic search.

⑤ Distance to Centroid

Distance to mean position of all items. Captures dispersion of the search.

Neurodegenerative Patients Show Erratic, Constricted Navigation

↑ Higher in patients

Distance to Next — larger semantic jumps
Velocity — erratic movement
Acceleration — abrupt direction changes
Entropy — unpredictable search

↓ Lower in patients

Distance to Centroid — search confined to a tight neighborhood despite being more volatile.

Interpretation: a kinematic signature of executive dysfunction — volatile trajectories within a diminished semantic space.

Swear Words & Cross-Linguistic Structure

Swear Words

Highest kinematic values across all metrics. Taboo lexicons lack sub-category structure → high variability in a compact space.

Animals

Lowest kinematic values, highest centroid distance. Structured sub-categories allow organized exploitation.

🇮🇹 🇩🇪 Cross-Linguistic

Italian and German datasets reveal language-specific category discrimination. Same protocol, different category effects — cultural and linguistic structure shapes semantic organization.

Different Models, Convergent Geometry

Inter-model agreement per metric (Pearson r across embedding models):

✓ Robust across models

Velocity, Acceleration, Distance-to-Next — high cross-model correlation. Local trajectory dynamics are encoder-invariant.

✓ Entropy: near-perfect

Depends on rank ordering, not absolute distances. Median binarization absorbs model differences.

⚠ Centroid: model-dependent

Static global average more sensitive to each model's high-level geometry. Potential tool for comparing how models structure knowledge.

→ Different models learn similar local dynamics despite different training pipelines and architectures (causal vs. bidirectional).

Cumulative vs. Non-Cumulative: Trajectory Length Matters

Cumulative → Long Sequences

Neurodegenerative (~20 items) · Swear Fluency (~21 items)

Longer trajectories provide rich context that cumulative embeddings leverage — more significant group differences and higher effect sizes.

Non-Cumulative → Short Sequences

Italian (~5 items) · German (~5.5 items)

With only ~5 items, there is too little context to accumulate. Point-to-point variation retains more discriminative signal.

Practical guideline: Use cumulative for ≥15 items (fluency tasks), non-cumulative for ≤6 items (property listing). Both approaches are complementary.

A Geometric Framework for Semantic Navigation

①

Cumulative trajectory metrics capture fine-grained navigation dynamics beyond binary clustering/switching.

②

Clinical utility — distinguishes neurodegenerative patients from controls via kinematic signatures of executive dysfunction.

③

Cross-linguistic & cross-domain — discriminates semantic categories and reveals language-specific organization.

④

Model-robust — convergent results across three embedding architectures for local dynamics.

Future directions: temporal timestamps · non-Euclidean metrics · richer information theory · applying the framework to LLMs — comparing human vs. artificial semantic navigation.
